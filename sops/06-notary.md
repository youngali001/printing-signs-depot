# SOP 06 — Notary Services

We provide **Florida notary public** services. A notary verifies the signer's
identity and willingness — **a notary does not verify that a document is true,
legal, or a good idea.** Only a commissioned notary on staff may notarize.

> Florida law governs every step here. When this SOP and Florida notary law
> conflict, **the law wins.** If you are not the commissioned notary, you cannot
> perform or "help with" the notarial act.

## 1. Before you start — the three checks

1. **Is a commissioned notary on duty?** If not, tell the customer when one is
   available; do not improvise.
2. **Is the signer physically present** in front of the notary? Required for
   in‑person notarization.
3. **Is the signer competent and willing?** They must appear aware and not
   coerced. If they seem confused, pressured, or impaired — **decline.**

## 2. Identify the signer

Acceptable ID is current (or recently expired per FL rules) government photo ID:
- FL driver license / state ID, U.S. passport, or other ID allowed under
  Florida law `[CONFIRM: maintain current acceptable‑ID list]`.
- The name on the ID must reasonably match the name on the document.
- If ID is insufficient, Florida allows credible‑witness identification — only
  if you understand and follow those rules; otherwise **decline**.

## 3. Examine the document

1. The document must be **complete** — no blank spaces meant to be filled
   (decline if blanks remain).
2. **Do not give legal advice** and do not choose the notarial certificate for
   the customer. Ask them which act they need (acknowledgment, jurat/oath,
   copy certification, etc.). If they don't know, they should ask the document's
   issuer or an attorney — not us.
3. The signer signs **in front of the notary** when the act requires it (e.g.,
   jurat). For an acknowledgment, the signer acknowledges the signature is
   theirs.

## 4. Perform the act

1. Administer the oath/affirmation if it's a jurat.
2. Complete the notarial certificate fully: venue (State of Florida, County of
   Hillsborough), date, signer name, act performed.
3. Affix signature and notary seal.
4. **Record the act in the notary journal** (see §5).

## 5. Notary journal

Maintain a journal entry for each act `[CONFIRM: matches current FL requirement
& store policy]`:
- Date and time
- Type of notarial act
- Type/title of document
- Signer's name and how identified (ID type, or witness)
- Fee charged

Keep the journal **secured and confidential** — it's locked up at close
([SOP 02](02-closing-procedures.md)).

## 6. Fees

- Florida caps the notary fee per signature/act `[CONFIRM: current FL maximum]`.
  Do not exceed it.
- Post the fee. Charge consistently. Add‑on services (printing, copies, witness
  coordination) are billed separately under their own SOPs.

## 7. When to decline (and how)

Decline politely if:
- No commissioned notary is on duty.
- Signer can't produce acceptable ID.
- Signer isn't present, seems impaired, confused, or pressured.
- Document is incomplete or you're asked to backdate, pre‑sign, or notarize
  something not signed in your presence.

Script: *"I'm sorry, I'm not able to notarize this one because [reason]. Here's
what would let me help: [fix]."* Never bend the rules to make a sale.

## 8. Witnesses

If a document needs witnesses (separate from notarization), we can sometimes
provide them `[CONFIRM: store policy & fee]`. A witness must actually observe the
signing. Don't witness something you didn't see signed.
