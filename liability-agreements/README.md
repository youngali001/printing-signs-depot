# Handyman Business — Liability Agreement System

A complete, plain-English set of customer agreements and disclaimers designed to
limit your handyman company's exposure to claims, disputes, and lawsuits — built
on the same risk-shifting principles that moving companies, contractors, and
home-service franchises use.

> ## ⚠️ READ THIS FIRST — NOT LEGAL ADVICE
>
> These documents are **templates / starting points**, not legal advice, and the
> author is not a lawyer. Liability caps, "as-is" releases, indemnification,
> warranty disclaimers, and mandatory-arbitration clauses are **regulated and
> their enforceability varies by state**. Some language below may be
> unenforceable, or even illegal, in your jurisdiction if used as-is.
>
> **Before any customer signs:** have a licensed attorney in your state (and for
> your trade) review and adapt these. Also confirm your contractor licensing,
> insurance (general liability + workers' comp), and bonding are in place — no
> contract substitutes for insurance.

## How the protection works (the strategy)

Moving companies don't avoid liability by being careful alone — they do it by
**contractually shifting and capping risk before the work starts**. The same five
levers run through every document here:

1. **Disclosure & inspection** — Document what existed before you arrived
   (pre-existing damage, hidden conditions) so you're not blamed for it.
2. **Limitation of liability** — Cap your maximum financial exposure (e.g., to
   the amount the customer paid for that job), and exclude *consequential*
   damages (the "your $40 repair failed and flooded my $40,000 basement" claim).
3. **Disclaimers & exclusions** — Carve out the things genuinely outside your
   control: concealed conditions, code violations in existing work, acts of God,
   customer-supplied materials, third-party products.
4. **Warranty containment** — Give a clear *limited* warranty so the customer
   knows exactly what's covered, instead of an implied promise of perfection.
5. **Dispute control** — Require written claims within a short window, choose
   your venue/governing law, and (where enforceable) require arbitration to avoid
   jury trials.

No single clause is bulletproof. Layered together and **actually signed**, they
move you from "easy to sue" to "expensive and unattractive to sue."

## Using these in practice

- **Get it signed BEFORE work begins.** A waiver signed after a problem is far
  weaker. Build signing into your booking/quote flow.
- **Fill in every `[BRACKET]`** — company name, license #, dollar amounts, state.
- **Don't bury it.** Courts disfavor hidden liability waivers. Use clear headings,
  readable type, and have the customer **initial the key limitation sections**.
- **Keep signed copies.** Store a countersigned PDF for every job (you have
  DocuSign available — these can be turned into reusable e-sign templates).
- **Be consistent.** Use the same documents every time; selective use undermines
  enforceability and looks like bad faith.
