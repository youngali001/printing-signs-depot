# SOP 11 — Customer Service, Claims & Problem Resolution

How we treat people is the business. Most of our services exist elsewhere — what
brings customers back is being **fast, accurate, and genuinely helpful.**

## 1. Service standards

- **Greet within 10 seconds.** Acknowledge a waiting line even while serving
  someone: *"I'll be right with you."*
- Be knowledgeable: know our services and prices, or know where to look.
- Move fast on the simple stuff (drop‑offs, copies) so the line stays short.
- Slow down and be thorough on the careful stuff (notary, passport, high‑value
  shipping).
- Thank every customer and invite them back.

## 2. Phones

1. Answer within `[CONFIRM: 3]` rings: *"Thank you for calling Cross Creek Pak N
   Ship, this is [name], how can I help you?"*
2. If you must place on hold, ask first and check back every ~30 seconds.
3. Take a clear message (name, number, what they need, time) if the right person
   is unavailable; ensure it gets to them.

## 3. Handling an upset customer (LEAP)

1. **Listen** — let them finish without interrupting.
2. **Empathize** — *"I understand why that's frustrating."*
3. **Apologize** — for the experience, sincerely, even if it wasn't our fault.
4. **Problem‑solve** — state what you *can* do and do it. If it's beyond your
   authority, get the manager rather than saying "no" and stopping.
- Never argue, never raise your voice. If a customer becomes abusive or
  threatening, calmly disengage and get the manager; call police if anyone's
  safety is at risk.

## 4. Lost / damaged / late package claims

1. **Stay calm and gather facts:** tracking number, ship date, service, declared
   value, who packed it.
2. Look up the tracking together; explain the current status honestly. Don't
   promise a delivery date the carrier doesn't guarantee.
3. **Late but moving:** explain estimated vs. guaranteed service; if it was a
   *guaranteed* service that failed, note possible refund eligibility and start
   the carrier process.
4. **Lost or damaged:**
   - Confirm whether insurance/declared value was purchased.
   - Start the **carrier's claim process** (USPS/UPS/FedEx/DHL) — claims are
     filed with the carrier, not paid from the register.
   - Claims usually require the **receipt, tracking, proof of value** (and for
     damage, photos and the packaging). Tell the customer what to bring.
   - If **we packed it** and it was insured, our packing supports the claim —
     pull our packing notes ([SOP 03](03-shipping-and-packing.md)).
5. Log the issue (date, customer, tracking, what we did) so the manager can
   follow up. Give the customer a realistic timeline and a way to reach us.

## 5. Refunds & service recovery

- Service errors that are **our fault** (wrong postage we entered, a bad print)
  → make it right promptly per [SOP 10](10-cash-handling-pos.md) refund limits.
- Don't refund carrier postage from our drawer — that goes through the carrier
  ([SOP 05](05-usps-cpu.md)).
- For anything beyond your authority, escalate to the manager **with the facts
  already gathered** so they can decide quickly.

## 6. Privacy & professionalism

- Customer documents, IDs, addresses, and shipment contents are **confidential.**
  Don't discuss one customer's business with another.
- Keep personal conversations and phones off the counter during service.

> Be fast where you can and careful where it counts. When something goes wrong:
> listen, own the experience, gather the facts, and start the right process — or
> get the manager. Never improvise a refund or a promise.
